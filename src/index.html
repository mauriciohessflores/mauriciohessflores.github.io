Computer vision scientist · PhD, Electrical and Computer Engineering

Mauricio Hess-Flores

I develop algorithms for accurate 3D reconstruction from images, and tools to see how much each result can be trusted. My work covers structure-from-motion, triangulation and bundle adjustment, photogrammetry from aerial and drone imagery, and geolocation and 3D mapping for NASA Earth and planetary science missions.

Over 20 years I have worked at UC Davis and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory and in industry. I am an Affiliate Scientist at the Space Science Institute and an IEEE Senior Member.

Research

Accurate multi-view geometry

How to recover 3D points and cameras accurately and quickly from many views. With students at UC Davis I developed angular-error cost functions for multi-view triangulation that are accurate and fast enough for very large scenes, including GPU versions. I studied how they behave under noise and short baselines, and used ray divergence to condition bundle adjustment.

Error and uncertainty visualization

Reconstruction pipelines rarely show why a result is wrong. I use scientific visualization to expose scene uncertainty, feature-track quality and sensitivity to each input. At JPL I was PI of a Caltech Data to Discovery award that produced VECTOR, an error-visualization tool now used in Mars 2020 operations (IEEE VIS 2024; code at NASA-AMMOS/VECTOR).

{{SELECTED:uncertainty}}

Aerial and large-scale reconstruction

My PhD and Lawrence Scholar work at LLNL addressed dense, accurate reconstruction from aerial video: detecting, separating and correcting correspondence and camera errors, parallax-path constraints, and frame decimation for streaming. The algorithms became part of a reconstruction pipeline at the laboratory. Later work added depth sensors and hybrid photogrammetry (CONTENT 2014 Best Paper Award).

Earth and planetary science

At NASA JPL I worked on Mars 2020 ground processing, instrument geolocation for Earth science missions, NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services, and atmospheric tomography from multi-angle measurements. At the Space Science Institute I am extending this to 3D reconstruction and calibration of planetary surfaces from archival mission imagery.
